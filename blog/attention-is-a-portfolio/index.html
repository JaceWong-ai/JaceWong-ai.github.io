Attention is a portfolio

Before we allocate capital, we allocate awareness. The first portfolio compounds into the second.

002
Begin

Field note / 002

Written by Jace Wong

Investing is usually described as the allocation of capital under uncertainty. The definition is accurate, but incomplete. Long before money moves, attention does. We decide which changes deserve observation, which ideas deserve study, and which signals deserve patience.

Seen this way, attention is the first portfolio. It has positions, concentrations, opportunity costs, and a time horizon. It can be diversified until it means nothing, or concentrated until the world outside the thesis disappears.

What compounds before money

A well-placed hour rarely announces its return. Reading a difficult book, following a technical shift, or understanding a company from first principles may look unproductive for a long time. Then one day, several quiet observations connect. The return arrives all at once, but the compounding did not.

“What we repeatedly notice becomes the world we are capable of acting in.

This is also why the loudest information is so expensive. It does not merely consume time; it changes the composition of the portfolio. A day spent reacting is a day not spent building a view.

Conviction without closure

Good investing needs conviction, but conviction is not the same as certainty. It is a willingness to act while keeping the model revisable. The same is true of a life of ideas: commit deeply, update honestly, and never confuse consistency with truth.

Capital follows attention. The more important question is whether our attention is already invested in the future we claim to believe in.
