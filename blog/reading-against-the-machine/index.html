Reading against the machine

In an age of instant synthesis, slow reading becomes a way to preserve intellectual texture.

003
Begin

Field note / 003

Written by Jace Wong

A machine can now summarize in seconds what takes us hours to read. This is useful. It is also a temptation to mistake the transfer of information for the formation of thought.

A summary gives us the shape of an argument. Reading gives us its weather: the hesitation, the rhythm, the examples that refuse to compress, the sentence that alters the meaning of the one before it. Information survives compression better than experience does.

Friction has a function

The difficulty of a book is not always a defect waiting to be optimized away. Sometimes friction is the mechanism. It slows the mind enough for unfamiliar structures to take hold.

“To read slowly is to let another mind change the pace of your own.

This does not make AI the enemy of reading. The better relationship is asymmetric: use machines to widen the field, then choose where to go deep without them. Let synthesis reveal the map; let attention decide where to walk.

Keep the long path

The future will make many cognitive shortcuts abundant. That abundance increases the value of knowing when not to take one. A person who can move quickly and still choose slowness holds an unusual advantage.

I read not because information is scarce, but because interiority is. The long path leaves traces that no summary can reproduce.
